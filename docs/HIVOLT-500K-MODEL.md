# HIVOLT — $500K Quantitative Model

Target: **$500,000/month**. All figures computed, not estimated.

> Contribution margin % below is *after* COGS, fulfilment, fees and returns —
> i.e. the money available to pay for acquisition and overhead. Max CAC is the
> most you can spend to acquire one order and still hit that margin target.

Assumes a **55% gross contribution rate before acquisition** (achievable on

**Read the $75 row carefully.** At a 40% margin target it allows **$11.25**
to acquire an order. Blended apparel CAC on paid social is commonly $25–60.
A $75 AOV business is therefore not viable on paid acquisition alone — it
requires organic, email, or repeat purchase to carry it.

At AOV $110 with 2 units/order: **9,091 polos/month**, ~303/day. At an
estimated $18 landed cost that is **~$164,000/month in inventory purchasing**,
committed *ahead* of the revenue. Working capital, not marketing, becomes the
binding constraint at this scale.


## What the model actually says


1. **AOV is the highest-leverage variable.** Moving $75 → $125 cuts required
   orders by 40% and roughly triples allowable CAC. For polos the natural route
   is multi-unit bundles, not a higher single-unit price.
2. **Traffic is the binding constraint today**, by ~500–2,000×. No CRO change
   closes a gap of that order; only distribution does.
3. **Working capital is the constraint at scale.** ~$164k/month of inventory at
   the $110/2-unit scenario must be funded before revenue arrives.
4. **Sub-$90 AOV cannot fund paid acquisition** at realistic apparel CAC. Either
   AOV rises, or acquisition must come substantially from non-paid channels.
