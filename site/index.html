The 220 argument

Why fabric weight is
the only number
that matters in a squat.

Grams per square metre is knit density. Below about 200 g/m², a legging stretched across the glute thins enough to go translucent — the failure everyone has had and nobody photographs. Our core knit is 220.

Questions worth asking

Including the ones most stores avoid

Are these your own designs, or blanks?

They're manufacturer blanks, chosen and specified by us — the same model most activewear labels under $100 use, including several you'd recognise. We don't claim to run a factory. What we do is publish the actual specification instead of hiding it behind words like "buttery" and "second-skin".

Why does shipping take 8–14 days?

Because that's how long it takes. Orders are picked and dispatched from a facility in Illinois. We could quote 3–5 days and disappoint most of you; we'd rather quote the real window and hit it.

There are no reviews on this site. Why?

Because HIVOLT is new and nobody has written one yet. Every review widget you've seen on a store this young was either imported from a supplier or bought. When we have real customers, we'll publish what they actually say, including the bad ones.

What does 220 g/m² actually mean for me?

Grams per square metre is how much a square metre of the fabric weighs — a direct proxy for knit density. Denser knit stretches less at the same load, which is why it stays opaque through a deep squat. Most leggings in this price range sit at 180–200 and don't publish the figure at all.

Do you ship outside the United States?

No. Not yet, and we'd rather say no than take an order we can't deliver properly. If that changes it'll be announced here first.
