# GOOGLE-ADS.md

Capture of the course's Google Ads section, as streamed. Started 2026-08-27.
The standing decision this doc sits under: **we do not run the ads ourselves** —
a Google-Ads media buyer does (onboarded around 500 spend, paid ~10% of spend;
both gates already captured from the roadmap module). This doc exists so the
store side — feed, titles, catalogue — knows what the ads layer expects.

---

## §1 The campaign stack, in the order the course actually uses it

| Priority | Campaign | Role | When |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | **Performance Max** | The all-round campaign, targets across Google surfaces. Runs alongside Shopping. | Day one — it is the $5/day warm-up campaign during the freeze. |
| 1 | **Google Shopping** | "Where the biggest money is made." Appears when buying-intent keywords are searched; the ad is your image + price, nothing else. | Day one. |
| 2 | **Search** | Brand awareness, retargeting, "stealing competitor traffic." | "More advanced stage" — explicitly later. Not at launch. |
| — | Video / Display | Exist; the course skips them. | Not used. |

(One campaign type was named but garbled in the transcript — likely Demand
Gen — and explicitly not covered "because we will not run the ads ourselves.")

The store-side consequence is the skill's rule 2 restated from the ads side:
**every Google ad is built from the feed.** Shopping and PMax have no creative
to fix — the product image, the title's keywords, and the price *are* the ad.
A media buyer cannot buy their way out of a weak feed.

## §2 Push vs pull — the one-line model

Meta is push (interrupt someone with no intent, win on creative, redesign
creatives forever, pay per impression). Google is pull (the buyer is already
searching, win on image + price + title keywords, pay per click). Convincing
someone who is already looking to buy is the whole edge.

## §3 Three clocks now, not two

FEED-CALENDAR §6 resolved two clocks. This module adds a third. All start at
GMC approval and run concurrently — they do not stack:

| Clock | Length | What it gates |
|---|---|---|
| GMC trust freeze | **4 weeks** | No new imports, no feed edits |
| Media-buyer gate | ~3 weeks | When scaling decisions begin |
| **Campaign warm-up (new)** | **2–3 weeks** | When revenue becomes readable — early inconsistency is *normal*, not a signal |

The practical point of the third clock: nobody judges ROAS, kills products, or
panics on quiet days inside the first 2–3 weeks. The draft triggers in
FEED-CALENDAR §7 all read windows longer than the warm-up.

## §4 The course's own performance claims — recorded as claims

Narrated over screenshots I cannot verify; kept because they calibrate
expectations, flagged because they are the course selling itself:

- Their example store: first sale 16 September, €108 total that day; "a little
  bit inconsistent" through late September (the warm-up); then ~€1k/day at the
  start of October to a claimed **€37k/day within 25 days**, at a claimed
  monthly-average **ROAS of 2.5–3**.
- What the narration did not show: ad spend, margin, refunds, or net profit.
  A revenue curve at ROAS 2.5–3 is consistent with a good business; it is not
  a P&L, whatever the video calls it.
- The Meta contrast offered: quick spikes possible ("5k days in week one"),
  then collapse, daily ROAS battles around ~1.5/breakeven, and standing ad
  account ban risk. Recorded as the course's characterization of its own past
  Meta stores.

Expectation this sets for our timeline: **no revenue judgment before week 3,
no scaling judgment before week 4** — which is exactly where the freeze ends
and the import batches begin (FEED-CALENDAR §4).
