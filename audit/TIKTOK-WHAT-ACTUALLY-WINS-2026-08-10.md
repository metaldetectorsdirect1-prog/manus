## The finding

**Not one of them is a product film.** Not one is typography. Not one is a
specification.

They are people. Faces, bodies, jokes, nostalgia, hauls, problems. The two
brand-owned accounts that make the list — Primark and Kapow Meggings — win on
product novelty shown *on a body*, not on information. The closest thing to a
spec claim in the whole set is Serena Neel's squat-proof test, and that is a
person putting the garment through a squat on camera, not a card that states a
number.

This directly contradicts the eight posts HIVOLT has live. They are silent
black typography — 230 g/m², a price ladder, a returns policy. On the evidence
above, that format has no precedent at scale in this niche. It is not that the
information is wrong. It is that TikTok's ranker runs on completion, and a
static card gives a viewer nothing to complete.

## The one encouraging row

`burpee.act` — 32,700 followers — pulled **1.4M and 1.2M views** on two posts.
A small brand account can break out here. Their content is Spanish-language
humour about leggings, shot on a person. Kapow at 44.5k did 19.8M.

Followers are not the gate. Format is.

## What this implies for HIVOLT

The spec proposition is a genuine differentiator and should not be abandoned —
but it has to be *demonstrated on a body*, not *stated on a card*. The
difference between "270 g/m² is the most opaque thing we sell" as a typography
slab and as a person doing a squat in the 200 next to the 270 is the difference
between 0 views and Serena Neel's 8.5 million.

That requires a person on camera. HIVOLT has no model, no creator, and no
photography of its own — the store's only images are supplier stills, and AI
renders of the actual garments are off the table because customers receive the
real thing. This is the constraint that has to be solved before any of the
content work compounds, and it is not solvable from inside a container.

The realistic options are ordinary ones: the owner films their own hands and
garments, a micro-creator is paid per video, or the supplier's on-model
photography is licensed. None of them are technical.

## On "millions of organic visitors"

Worth stating plainly against the numbers above. Blogilates is a fifteen-year
brand built by a person with 3.7M followers. Isabel Clancy has 4.4M. Those are
not marketing outcomes, they are audiences built over years.

The reachable near-term target is the Burpee row: a small account, one video
that breaks out, low tens of thousands of visits. That is worth chasing and it
is a different plan from chasing millions. The store currently takes 10–18 real
human visitors a day.
