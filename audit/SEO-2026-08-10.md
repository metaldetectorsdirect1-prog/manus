# SEO pass — 2026-08-10

## Fixed

| Surface | Was | Now |
|---|---|---|
| `leggings` collection | no meta description | weight range 200–270 g/m², published-weight hook |
| `outerwear-hoodies` collection | no meta description | 320–380 g/m² layers |
| `drop-04-voltcore` collection | **no SEO title** | "Drop 04 Voltcore — Matte Black 220 g/m² Sets" |
| `voltcore-2-piece-set…` product | **no SEO title** | title with the 220 g/m² spec in it |

The Voltcore product is the store's **#2 landing page** — 82 sessions in 14
days, behind only the homepage — and it had no SEO title, so Google was
inventing one from the H1.

## A trap that cost a field for about sixty seconds

`SEOInput` on `productUpdate` **nulls whatever you omit**. Sending only
`seo: {title: …}` wiped the product's existing meta description; the mutation
response is what showed `description: null` a moment after it had held real
copy. Restored in the next call.

**Always send `title` and `description` together**, on both `productUpdate` and
`collectionUpdate`, even when only one is changing. The three collection edits
above happened to pass both, so they were never at risk — that was luck rather
than care.

## One claim removed

Voltcore's meta description said **"Squat-proof, save $13."** The product
carries no `compareAtPrice`, so there is no reference price to save against —
the figure had nothing behind it. Replaced with the actual price. "Squat-proof"
also went: it is a performance claim the store cannot substantiate, and the
whole proposition is that the number is stated rather than the adjective.

## A contradiction that turned out not to be one

Four leggings showed **$39.99 against published weights of 220 and 230 g/m²**,
while active 220s sell at $54 and the 230 at $59. That would have broken the
price ladder outright, and the `ladder` film published last night states the
ladder as fact.

They are all `status: ARCHIVED` — not purchasable, not published. **The ladder
holds across every active product**, exactly as recorded:

| g/m² | Price | Active products |
|---|---|---|
| 200 | $49 | 1 |
| 220 | $54 | 5 |
| 230 | $59 | 1 |
| 250 | $64 | 1 |
| 270 | $69 | 1 |

Worth writing down because the check is cheap and the alternative was
announcing a defect that does not exist. Archived products still appear in
collection queries — always read `status` before comparing prices.

## The duplicate meta descriptions — now closed

Several active products had carried an auto-generated meta description: the
plain text of the whole product page, size table included, ending " - HIVOLT".
The first 155 characters were **identical boilerplate across the range** —
"built for training that asks for range: squats, lunges, floor work and the
walk home" repeating verbatim on every legging. That was a duplicate signal on
exactly the pages that should compete.

Re-checked every active product on 2026-08-10 by paging `products(query:
"status:active")` and reading `seo { title description }`. **All of them now
carry a distinct, hand-written description**, and almost every one is led by
the figure that differs — 220 g/m², 270 g/m², 330 g/m², 91 g/m². No boilerplate
survives.

## The blog is not the gap either

Worth writing down because the previous note implied otherwise. `/blogs/news`
("Training Journal") holds **500 published articles**, and a sample of fifty
shows each with its own `global.title_tag` and `global.description_tag`. The
bodies are real: internal links to specific products and collections, `Article`
and `FAQPage` JSON-LD, and — the part that matters here — the actual published
fabric weights used as the argument rather than adjectives.

`/blogs/learn` still exists with zero articles, orphaned from every menu. One
thin URL in the sitemap. Left alone rather than deleted, because a theme
section may reference the handle and the storefront cannot be fetched from this
container to check.

## What that leaves

On-page SEO is, as far as the Admin API can see, done: titles, descriptions,
collection copy, 500 articles, internal linking, structured data. The store
still takes 10–18 real human visitors a day.

That gap is not an on-page problem and no further on-page work will close it.
What is left is off-page and owner-gated — the Search Console token is still
unpasted, so **nothing here is being measured against real query data**, and
theme v33 is still unpublished. Until those two happen, further SEO edits are
guesses being made in the dark, however well-reasoned.
